Why take part

Benefits of the Talent Hunt

SGTH 2026 is designed to give young learners a meaningful stage to discover, express, and grow their abilities.

  • Exciting Opportunities

    A platform for students to showcase their talent, curiosity, and creativity beyond everyday classroom learning.

  • Assured Rewards

    Effort and talent deserve recognition. Rewards are assured as part of the talent hunt experience.

  • Structured Guidance

    Thoughtful direction from Student Guide helps every student understand where they shine and how to grow further.

  • Merit-Based Scholarships

    Outstanding performance is recognised through scholarships awarded on merit, encouraging students to aim higher.

EVS & General Science

11 topics

One common syllabus for Classes III–V. Difficulty is set through question design and application level rather than separate class-wise syllabi. Tap a topic to see what it covers.

  1. Living World

    Living and non-living things; plants and their parts; functions of roots, stems, leaves, flowers and fruits; animals and birds; animal homes and habitats; basic adaptation and characteristics of animals.

  2. Human Body, Food & Health

    Major parts of the human body; sense organs; bones, muscles and basic body functions; food and nutrients; balanced diet; personal hygiene and cleanliness; common diseases and prevention; healthy habits.

  3. Air, Water & Soil

    Importance and uses of air; sources and uses of water; water cycle – basic understanding; water conservation; water pollution; types and uses of soil; importance of soil for plants and life.

  4. Environment & Natural Resources

    Our environment; natural and man-made resources; renewable and non-renewable resources – basic awareness; pollution and its types; Reduce, Reuse and Recycle; conservation of natural resources.

  5. Earth, Sun, Moon & Space

    Earth and its shape; rotation and revolution – basic concepts; day and night; Sun, Moon and stars; Solar System; planets and their basic characteristics; natural satellite.

  6. Weather & Seasons

    Weather and climate – basic understanding; seasons; rain, wind and clouds; changes in weather; importance of weather in daily life.

  7. Matter, Energy & Everyday Science

    Solids, liquids and gases; changes in materials; uses and properties of common materials; light and shadows; heat; sound; push and pull; simple machines and tools; sources of energy.

  8. Our Surroundings & Community

    Family and neighbourhood; school and community; occupations; public places and services; basic civic sense; clean and safe surroundings.

  9. Safety & Daily Life

    Road safety; traffic signs – basic awareness; fire and electrical safety; first aid – basic knowledge; safe and unsafe situations; emergency awareness.

  10. Transport & Communication

    Means of transport; land, water and air transport; traditional and modern communication; telephone, internet, post, radio and television; uses of technology in everyday life.

  11. Observation, Reasoning & Application

    Picture-based identification; observation and classification; similarities and differences; cause-and-effect questions; everyday-life situations; simple logical application; “What would happen if…?” questions; diagram/picture-based questions.

About Student Guide

Every young mind holds a spark

At Student Guide, we believe every young mind holds an extraordinary spark of potential. Our mission is to move beyond traditional rote memorisation in the classroom by discovering, recognising, and celebrating raw talent among junior school students.

Where curiosity meets opportunity.

Our Vision

“We believe that intelligence is a journey, not a fixed score.”

The talent hunt encourages students to stretch their boundaries, make logical connections, and explore their potential beyond memorisation.

  • Cognitive growth

    Building the thinking skills that help young learners understand ideas deeply, not just recall them.

  • Intellectual agility

    Encouraging quick, flexible thinking and the confidence to approach unfamiliar questions.

  • Creative thinking

    Celebrating imagination and original ideas as essential parts of how children learn.

  • Real-world problem solving

    Connecting learning to everyday challenges so students can apply what they know with logic and care.
